## Test definition

Question A: Does adding nearby reconditioning/inventory improve local Carvana retail share? Question B: Is the improvement larger when it materially reduces distance to the nearest existing reconditioning site? The second question directly tests whether proximity, rather than simply adding throughput, matters.

Use buyer ZIP/county-month retail sales or registrations, not sales assigned to a dealer license. Primary outcome is Carvana used-retail units divided by local used-retail units with matched definitions. Secondary outcome is units per adult population if a reliable industry denominator is unavailable. No free validated local Carvana retail series has yet been obtained; the roster does not solve the sales-data gap.

Define exposure using distance/travel time to the nearest operating retail reconditioning/inventory site, including original IRCs. Identify the change in nearest-site distance when an ADESA integration becomes operational. Pickup/vending locations, delivery hubs, auction-only sites and reconditioning centers are separate exposures. A pickup location can still affect trust or convenience and should not be silently ignored.

Illustrative starting bands, to be tested rather than treated as established service boundaries: 0–50, 50–100, 100–200 and over 200 road miles. First map actual addresses and pre-existing sites; no ZIP assignment or distance calculation has yet been performed. Avoid straight-line map dots masquerading as verified facilities.

Construct three local groups: (1) newly close to a reconditioning site after integration; (2) already close to an existing IRC, receiving extra capacity; (3) no comparable change in proximity over the event window. Compare changes before/after against similar markets, not raw sales levels. Large urban markets were more likely to get sites and naturally sell more cars. The rest of the nation is a descriptive benchmark, not automatically a valid control.

Measure within-market changes over 12–24 pre-event months and up to 12 post-event months where available; report shorter windows for recent sites. Use not-yet-integrated markets as controls only before their own integrations. Check pre-trends, overlapping site catchments, adjacent-market displacement, new pickup hubs and regional economic conditions. Customers can buy nationally, so effects may cross the distance bands.

Aggregate treated and control outcomes with fixed pre-event weights or sum units/sum local-market totals. Count each buyer market once even when multiple sites overlap. Do not average market growth rates equally or compare one bucket with greater baseline population to another without normalization. Report first-nearby-site effects separately from capacity additions in already-served markets.

The total integration effect includes induced advertising; controlling for that advertising can remove part of the effect of interest. Separate total local sales lift from the narrower physical-delivery mechanism, and distinguish either from companywide incremental sales if neighboring markets lose share.
