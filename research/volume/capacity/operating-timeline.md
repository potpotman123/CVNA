## Capacity definitions do not make a utilization series

- **>1m original-network capacity (Q4 2024 call):** management's potential throughput with execution/hiring, not demonstrated production.
- **1.5m (Q4 2025 call; Q3 2026 letter):** Q4 call says investments already made in facilities; Q3 wording is current footprint's fully built-out annual capacity. Do not silently treat either as verified currently installed, staffed output. The scope of remaining equipment/buildout embedded in this language is not reconciled publicly.
- **3m real-estate capacity:** future output supported by property with additional construction and operations. Not a forecast or near-term available line capacity.
- **~13k / ~40k additions:** generic incremental annual capacity for integrations/full buildouts. Site mix, commissioning, staffing and first-year availability remain separate assumptions.
- **>800k annualized threshold:** four times Q3's >200k production disclosure; useful only as a lower-bound normalization. No exact production utilization computed against 1.5m or 3m.

## Stock-flow reconciliation

For a consistent owned, sale-ready population:

`Ending ready inventory = beginning ready inventory + newly completed vehicles + ready acquisitions/returned vehicles − owned-ready retail deliveries − wholesale transfers/other removals`.

Marketplace units generally require a separate population bridge. A return may increase ready inventory or return to repair work; do not count it in both. Cars listed before being ready are outside this ready-stock definition. Internal transfers between sites cancel nationally. The public data do not identify enough of these terms to solve the equation. Q3 production slightly exceeding sales is directionally consistent with rebuilding supply, but is not a measured ready-inventory increase.

Balance-sheet vehicle inventory increased from $2.408bn at December 2025 to $3.456bn at September 2026 (+$1.048bn). This is a dollar stock containing cost/mix and potentially different vehicle stages/populations; it cannot be converted directly into ready units or site turns. The cash-flow inventory use is $1.025bn, a different financial measure. The $23m difference is not forced into a unit reconciliation.

Activations precede portions of the growth, but disclosed production, sales, marketing and vehicle allocation respond to one another. The record establishes a sequence consistent with an enabling supply-chain expansion; it does not identify whether each new production unit caused a sale or was produced in response to stronger demand.
