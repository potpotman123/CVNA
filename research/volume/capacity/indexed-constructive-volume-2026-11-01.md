# Indexed constructive volume case — November 1, 2026

The 15% staffing scenario is an explicit constructive case, not a proven base forecast. It was originally a sensitivity; historical evidence provides a plausibility check, not a statistical derivation.

## Calibration

The matched 14-original-IRC panel averaged 4,356 employees in 2023, 5,048 in 2024 (+15.9%), and 6,806 in 2025 (+34.8%). Thus 15% annual expansion is within demonstrated staffing growth rates. However, 2025 employment remained 5.6% below 2022: much of this was recovery, and headcount growth does not equal growth in trained effective hours. Haines/Elyria tours support additional shifts and lines but do not quantify the remaining portfolio ramp. Sources and population definitions: [matched staffing panel](staffing-test-results-2026-10-30.md), [scenario methodology](existing-network-ramp-2026-11-01.md).

## Explicit forecast

Index the original facilities to 100 at the Q3 2026 midpoint. Grow effective hours 15% annually with unchanged output per effective hour, stop at 1m original units, and average monthly rates into calendar-year volumes. Continued staffing replaces the old historical-recovery calculation. Preserve all ADESA cohorts and consensus wholesale.

## What must be true

By 2029 the case requires approximately 49% more effective original-site hours than the Q3 proxy, around 3,830 additional establishment FTE equivalents at the assumed 2,000 hours per year. These are net productive equivalents, not gross hires, technician counts or a measured staffing plan. Distributed evenly only for scale, that is about 213 extra equivalents per original site by 2029; actual allocations must reflect local bottlenecks. Recruiting must also replace attrition and train workers.

At constant productivity, 2029 annual-average production reaches roughly 96.5% of the assumed 1m cap. That is a demanding ramp, not a conservative assumption merely because headcount grew faster once. Staffing also competes with new ADESA sites for managers and skilled hires. Financing concessions, sourcing and aged inventory can constrain profitable absorption.

The 2027 lead is only 4,826 cars, or 0.46%: too small to treat as a robust variant view. The 2028/29 differences are more material. The supplied consensus ends in 2029, so no claim is made about outperforming consensus in later years. No new EBITDA or price target is generated; existing cost sensitivities and normal-labor double-counting precautions still apply.

## Model decision

Use this as the constructive volume case and retain 5%/10% execution scenarios. It can support a conditional long argument, but the exact 15% remains an analyst assumption. Promote it to a base forecast only alongside demand, staffing execution and cost evidence; do not claim the tours prove this growth rate.
