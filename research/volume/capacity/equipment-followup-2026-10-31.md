## 1. Tolleson: a specifically identified bottleneck

In the March 2, 2026 Morgan Stanley conference transcript, Garcia identifies Tolleson and says paint booths have tended to constrain it. His example links paint-staff absences to the need for reassignment, queues, idle labor in other stages and quality-control failures when workers subsequently rush vehicles. Manager software is intended to improve these allocation decisions.

This is specific management testimony supporting a labor-allocation/process constraint. It does not disclose booth utilization, output lost, repair mix or improvement achieved. It cannot show whether current output requires additional equipment. Pair it with the historical supplier installation evidence without treating the two sources as a current equipment audit.

## 2. Indianapolis/Greenfield: installed equipment documented by contractor

James Babcock's project account dates electrical work completion to April 2019. It describes a 90,000 sq ft inspection/service/photo building and a separate 40,000 sq ft paint facility. The paint building had **eight booths, 35 prep positions and eight lifts**, with a new 1,200-amp service. The service building had 18 inspection areas. Do not confuse the eight lifts in the paint building with all lifts at the site.

The listed $2.7m project budget pertains to this contractor's electrical/fire-alarm/communications scope, not total IRC development cost. Existing infrastructure was reused where possible. This provides a historical installed-equipment baseline, not a current booth count or production rate. [Contractor account](https://www.jamesbabcockinc.com/carvana-indianapolis-facility/).

Carvana's March 2019 opening announcement described 130,000 sq ft and approximately 500 employees at full capacity. That was an opening-era staffing expectation, not an immutable capacity denominator. [Company release](https://www.carvana.com/press/carvana-strengthens-midwest-operations-with-indianapolis-inspection-center/).

The previously collected OSHA panel for Greenfield reports:

| Year | Annual average employees | Total reported hours |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2022 | 735 | 1,293,836 |
| 2023 | 467 | 843,912 |
| 2024 | 481 | 734,132 |
| 2025 | 565 | 1,110,342 |

Derived 2024–25 changes: headcount **+17.5%**, hours **+51.2%**. Relative to 2022: headcount **−23.1%**, hours **−14.2%**. The apparent staffing gap is therefore materially smaller when expressed as labor hours. But 2025 adds another address and changes industry/ZIP coding: population expansion or reporting differences could contribute. These are reported total hours, not paint hours, productive hours or mix-adjusted output.

This is a countercheck to the bullish argument: comparing 565 employees with 735 alone overstates the measured labor-input shortfall relative to an hours comparison. Comparing 565 with the old 500 target would absurdly suggest over-100% utilization. Neither ratio measures factory utilization.

## 3. Trenton: equipment predates the later staffing shortfall

February 17, 2022 council minutes record the mayor's ribbon-cutting description: **12 paint booths, 91 lifts and three photo domes**. This is a contemporaneous municipal account, not an engineering equipment schedule. It also reports 400 employees and an anticipated 1,000; these staffing statements conflict in definition/timing with later annual and incentive figures and must not become utilization denominators. [Minutes, PDF p5](https://trentonoh.gov/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_02172022-203).

Already-known May 15, 2025 minutes record Carvana's representative reporting **222 FTEs**, more than **65 openings**, and **$49.9m invested** versus **$24m committed**. The explanation included industry conditions and lower-than-planned scaling. These are attributed company representations within municipal minutes, not audited equipment spending. [May minutes, pp1–2](https://www.ci.trenton.oh.us/AgendaCenter/ViewFile/Minutes/_05152025-472).

The new connection is chronological: substantial installed machinery existed before the later employment shortfall. This supports separating physical investment from operational activation, but does not establish spare capacity today, a paint-staff count or the output obtainable from filling vacancies. May FTEs and the existing 2025 OSHA annual average of 310 employees are different populations/periods.

## 4. What remains blocked; how this affects the thesis

Exact-identifier and alternate-index searches did not recover the contents of Elyria permits P0140559/P0140630. No equipment changes inferred. No new verified Belton commissioning or matched site output emerged. An OSHA enforcement record mentioning two paint lines lacked a verified site match in this pass and was excluded.

The evidence now supports a sharper mechanism: installed equipment can coexist with inconsistent allocation of skilled labor, causing reduced flow and higher cost. That is different from simply having empty floor space. It also cautions against assuming every unfilled job creates incremental output or every historical staffing gap represents unused capacity.

**Model-ready:** historical equipment counts as dated context; source-defined labor inputs with scope flags; Tolleson's named management explanation as qualitative evidence. **Not model-ready:** current utilization, production elasticity to hires, or additional profitable units. No numerical production-growth forecast changed.

Next discriminating evidence would be current paint staffing/booth schedules paired with completed output and repair mix, or a second dated operating observation at one of these equipment-documented sites. More national job counts or aerial parking occupancy would not identify that relationship.
