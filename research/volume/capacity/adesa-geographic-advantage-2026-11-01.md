# ADESA placement versus the legacy IRC network

Added volume-thesis point, November 1, 2026. ADESA was acquired in May 2022; the recent events here are retail integrations, not new property acquisitions. This is a targeted comparison, not a representative geographic census or measured sales uplift.

## Finding

The strongest supported claim is **better-distributed local inventory and access to additional hiring catchments**, not that every ADESA site is nearer downtown than every original IRC. Some integrations create a new local production pool; others complement an existing IRC on a different side of a metro. Both can increase the selection eligible for fast delivery. A customer already eligible for same-day delivery may gain more eligible vehicles, even if the fastest advertised delivery time does not change.

## Fresh primary-source observations

1. **Kansas City: delivery capability followed the integration announcement.** On September 30, 2024, Carvana announced same-day delivery for select local customers and explicitly linked local ready inventory to Belton. This is 42 calendar days after the August 19 integration announcement, not a measured construction/ramp duration. It is the clearest selected example connecting geography to a changed delivery offering. No buyer-local sales effect is disclosed. [Integration](https://press.carvana.com/carvana-develops-first-auction-irc-megasite-in-kansas-city/) · [Delivery launch](https://investors.carvana.com/news-releases/2024/09-30-2024-100009412).

2. **Chicago: better network coverage, not first same-day delivery.** The May 2026 release says Chicago same-day delivery launched the previous year. It describes 75 acres, nearly 6,000 parking spaces and approximately 100 planned jobs over time. These are footprint/job-plan figures, not output or filled employment. The new inventory pool can broaden fast-delivery selection in an already-served metro. [Integration release](https://press.carvana.com/carvana-brings-inspection-and-reconditioning-center-capabilities-to-adesa-chicago/).

3. **Chicago's placement was deliberately chosen for transport access.** ADESA's 2015 development records put the site off I-90, near O'Hare and among six major interstate systems. The original plan developed 65 acres with an option to expand to 150; those are historical development/option figures, not the current 75-acre description. The release explicitly calls it a greenfield development. This contradicts treating all acquired ADESA sites as decades-old, grandfathered inner-city land. [April 2015 development release](https://www.adesa.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/150409-KAR-Auction-Services-Announces-Development-of-ADESA-Chicago.pdf) · [September 2015 groundbreaking](https://www.adesa.com/wp-content/uploads/2019/02/151230-ADESA-Breaks-Ground-on-New-Chicago-Auction.pdf).

4. **Seattle: established infrastructure plus a local inventory pool.** The July 2025 release describes Auburn as a 40-acre, 4,000-space site operating for over four decades, with approximately 100 planned jobs over time. This supports reuse of established infrastructure and local recruiting. It does not verify grandfathered zoning, driving-time savings or a quantified available labor pool. [Company release](https://investors.carvana.com/news-releases/2025/07-28-2025-140020744).

5. **Atlanta: a second side of the metro with existing logistics infrastructure.** The January 2025 release calls Fairburn a South Fulton logistics hub, describes over 100 acres, almost 12,000 parking spaces and over 30 years of auction operation, and anticipates approximately 200 jobs over time. It promises more locally available fast-delivery inventory. This supports infrastructure reuse and geographic complementarity, not a measured city-center distance advantage. [Company release](https://press.carvana.com/carvana-announces-auction-irc-megasite-integration-in-atlanta-area/).

## How this enters the thesis

- **Demand channel:** more vehicles locally prepared means a larger subset of the national catalog can be delivered quickly. That can improve the offering without changing its advertised fastest delivery tier.
- **Cost channel:** shorter positioning/delivery distances can reduce transport expense, subject to load factors, routing and site costs. Do not translate unmeasured distance savings into a margin uplift.
- **Labor channel:** a second location can access workers unwilling or unable to commute to the legacy site. This is a plausible catchment mechanism; no commuting-zone or technician-supply study was completed.
- **Site-selection advantage:** reusing established auction properties can provide suitable access and infrastructure. The general legacy-zoning claim remains analyst testimony pending site-specific legal evidence; Chicago shows it is not universal.

Existing May 2024 company evidence already placed last-mile operations at 30 ADESA properties and middle-mile at nine. Therefore, all delivery improvements cannot be attributed to the later start of retail recon. [Prior source register](sources.md).

## Boundaries and next useful measurement

Company announcements and their syndications are not independent confirmations. This pass retrieved original releases and an acquisition-predecessor development record, rather than another sales/traffic analysis. No geocoding, road routing, population overlay or local sales estimate was performed. The Houston release timed out on this retrieval; its identity/date remain supported by the pre-existing source register rather than a newly read body.

If more precision is needed, compare drive-time catchments around paired original/ADESA sites using customer-population and worker-residence distributions; downtown distance alone is a poor measure. Include pre-existing delivery hubs and earlier ADESA integrations. Keep that separate from evidence that customers actually buy more at acceptable margins.

Pitch wording: “ADESA improves more than factory capacity: it adds local inventory in previously remote markets and places additional production on underserved sides of existing metros. Kansas City's same-day launch provides a concrete delivery example; Chicago illustrates broader fast-delivery selection and a separate hiring catchment rather than first-time market access.”
