# Volume thesis: production expansion, labor development and economics

Working thesis as of November 1, 2026. This is the central argument and evidence ledger for the volume build, not a completed profitability model or a proven causal decomposition. User requested documentation before further modeling.

## The argument we are underwriting

Carvana can potentially sell more vehicles by making more desirable inventory ready for sale and easier to deliver. Its existing properties and ADESA network provide room for expansion. The executable constraint is often trained labor, management and balanced workflows rather than acquiring another parcel of land. Restoring and expanding productive hours, maturing recently hired workers and ramping ADESA integrations could therefore support further volume growth without repeating the original property acquisition cycle.

Our working demand assumption is that customers absorb additional suitable inventory at acceptable economics. This is conditional, not “every car produced automatically sells.” The investment thesis requires both production expansion and profitable sales absorption.

## 1. Why believe production can enable sales, beyond correlated production and sales?

| Evidence/mechanism | What it contributes | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| More ready inventory supplies additional combinations of model, age, mileage, price and condition | A buyer who previously found no suitable car has a better chance of finding one; variety can increase conversion | Mechanism, not a measured causal elasticity; excess undesirable inventory will not help |
| Nearby recon inventory shortens delivery distance and time | Better customer convenience and potentially lower delivery expense/fees can make the offering more attractive | Delivery changes, financing and advertising move together; local incremental sales are unmeasured |
| Q3 2024 company discussion: approximately 300 fewer shipping miles in integrated-site metros | Specific operational evidence connecting ADESA placement to the customer offering | Company-reported; not independent proof of incremental demand |
| Q4 2025 ramp-related recon cost problems, early-2026 cost-first response, then renewed inventory expansion | Shows real operating tradeoffs affecting the pace at which inventory can be prepared | Management may also respond to demand; production is not an exogenous experiment |
| Haines City tour: line/shift expansion, operating improvements and output growing faster than labor | Facility-level evidence that process/staffing changes can raise completed output | No matched numerical before/after series; does not itself establish sell-through |
| Q2/Q3 2026 newer-car and prime-customer mix | A concrete channel through which easier preparation and a more attractive financed offering can jointly grow units | Confounds productivity with mix and financing; may not generalize to older/cheaper cars |

Taken together these support the plausibility of operational supply constraints relative to management's desired growth. They do not isolate how much historical sales growth came from supply versus demand, prices, credit terms, advertising or market share. There is no validated free buyer-local sales panel or causal local-site elasticity. Do not repeat website traffic analysis as if it closes this gap.

## 2. Why believe meaningful production headroom remains?

### A. Historical labor-hours recovery exists, but is uneven

The 14 matched original IRCs recorded 13.02m hours in 2022 and 12.68m in 2025. **The aggregate is only about 2.6% below 2022, not “far below.”** Some sites have expanded well above 2022 while others remain below it. Restoring each lagging site to its own 2022 hours without shrinking the growers produces 14.81m hours: **+2.13m / +16.8%** versus 2025.

That site-by-site recovery is the relevant evidence. Historical hours are not an efficient staffing target, a physical ceiling or proof that the same workers can be rehired. The 2025 baseline is stale relative to late 2026; some recovery may already be realized. Two-year recovery from 2025 means 2026–2027.

See [reproducible calculations](labor-recovery-calculations-2026-11-01.json) and [calculation handoff](labor-recovery-handoff-2026-11-01.md).

### B. Installed infrastructure sometimes precedes staffing

Examples include historical Greenfield paint equipment, Trenton's booths/lifts and subsequent below-plan staffing, and Haines City's additional line/second-shift ramp. These support the existence of staffing and shift opportunities inside existing facilities. They do not establish current spare equipment at every site or equal output per facility.

### C. ADESA broadens the expansion routes and hiring catchments

Integrations can adapt existing buildings; major buildouts add infrastructure on owned land. Company benchmarks distinguish approximately $2–3m / 13k incremental annual capacity for an integration from $30–35m / 40k for a full buildout. Access to separate local labor pools and closer inventory can make geographically distributed capacity useful even when older sites are not full. Budgets exclude the original acquisition and do not measure actual staffed output.

The candidate integration roster progresses from six in 2024 to 16 in 2025 and 21 through Q3 2026. Announcement dates are imperfect operating-date proxies. Three major buildouts were discussed for production beginning in 2027. See [candidate roster](../adesa-21-site-candidate-roster.json), [facility register](facility-register.json) and [sources](sources.md).

### D. Worker development may add a second benefit after hiring

Company employment and the matched IRC panel show broad rebuilding in 2024–2025. Citizens reports a roughly 1–3-year certification pathway. Retained cohorts may improve bottleneck skills, cross-coverage, quality and supervisory capacity during 2025–2028. This is plausible upside to constant output per hour, not a measured uplift or proof of a sudden 2026 inflection. See [training-cohort hypothesis](training-cohort-thesis-2026-11-01.md).

### E. Workflow improvements have specific mechanisms

Paint staffing/attendance, preparation queues, sequencing, parts availability and repeat work can prevent installed equipment from producing at its design rate. Better execution can increase usable throughput. Management's early-2026 HPU recovery and the Haines tour support scope for improvement; some gains are merely recovery of lost efficiency. Easier vehicle mix must be separated from within-category improvement. Adding labor away from the bottleneck may only increase expense.

### F. What the parking/physical work actually showed

**We did not complete a representative national parking-lot census or establish that a large share of factory capacity is unused.** Haines City's roughly 40% figure refers to parking occupancy, not production utilization. The Dayton municipal pilot verified a proposed roughly 68k-square-foot recon building replacing roughly 350 parking spaces: evidence of a physical expansion option requiring construction, not idle staffed production capacity. Management's roughly 50% network-utilization claim has an unclear denominator and should remain attributed.

See [physical headroom pilot](headroom-pilot-2026-10-31.md) and [equipment follow-up](equipment-followup-2026-10-31.md).

## 3. Current volume arithmetic and assumptions

The base simplification is constant labor hours per acceptable completed vehicle, with stable mix and adequate equipment/downstream capacity. This gives no future productivity credit. It is conservative about productivity, not a guarantee that the entire scenario is conservative.

Working conversational calculation, not a completed spreadsheet or observed site decomposition:

| Group | Estimated 2025 starting output, thousands | Conditional 2027 output, thousands |
|---|---:|---:|
| 14 matched original IRCs | 436 | 509 |
| Four other originals | 68 | 68 |
| Six 2024 ADESA integrations | 64 | 78 |
| Ten 2025 integrations | 30 | 130 |
| Seven assumed 2026 integrations | 0 | 73 |
| Three 2027 full buildouts | 0 | 48 |
| Total, unrounded calculation | 596.641 | 905.430 |

Starting company sales are used as an explicitly approximate production normalization; marketplace units and inventory changes prevent identity with internal production. ADESA output is estimated by assuming a linear 12-month ramp to 13k annual capacity from announcement months (month-midpoint approximation). The seven 2026 sites comprise five Q3 candidates plus two assumed November/December starts. Original output is the remaining 2025 normalization, allocated by reported hours across 18 originals; equal output per establishment hour is assumed. Full buildouts contribute 40% of 120k in their opening year. Future integrations beyond that cohort are excluded.

These assumptions produce roughly +52% versus 2025, not doubling. The four unmatched original sites (University Park, Trenton, Tooele, West Memphis) are flat only because no matched historical gap was established. Applying the 14-site 16.8% rate to them as a peer proxy adds approximately 11.3k, yielding roughly 917k. That alternative is not the baseline and is not observed headroom.

This arithmetic has not independently validated the ramp assumptions. Q3 2026 sales annualize to about 870k, so the 905k 2027 case is restrained relative to recent sales pace and may understate existing-site expansion. Do not present it as a calibrated forecast. Calendar-year output and exit run rates are different. The earlier ~100k original-IRC uplift obtained by applying 16.8% to all company sales was incorrect and is superseded.

## 4. Economics: what we still have to calculate

We have **not** established “the economic effects are good.” The hypothesis is that contribution from more sales plus selective fixed-cost leverage outweighs hiring, ramp, logistics, selling and capital costs.

| Component | Conservative treatment in a future build |
|---|---|
| Vehicle acquisition cost | Scale with volume and sourcing/mix; no automatic cheaper purchasing |
| Direct recon labor and materials | Constant hours per comparable car; wages, overtime, training and raises explicit; no automatic unit labor saving |
| Recon fixed costs | Allocate across more completed cars only where costs actually remain fixed; allow step-ups for shifts/sites |
| Logistics and customer service | Include variable costs and expansion steps; do not assume all are fixed |
| Advertising and other SG&A | Separate volume-related spend from corporate fixed costs; no blanket flat-SG&A assumption |
| Financing and ancillary contribution | Keep unit economics separate from recon improvements; stress compression rather than assuming unchanged exceptional gains |
| Capex and depreciation | Separate integrations, buildouts, maintenance and timing; no double counting gross project capacity or spend |
| Working capital | More vehicle stock requires cash; shorter queues can help, but are not synonymous with more output |

Respect reported accounting classification when mapping these economic costs to COGS or SG&A. Inventory carries recon costs until sale; production-period improvement can reach reported gross profit later. Incremental contribution equals incremental sales proceeds and related contribution less acquisition, preparation, delivery, selling and service costs; then subtract added fixed costs, depreciation and financing/tax effects as applicable. Do not apply current average company profit per vehicle as a marginal recon-site margin.

Important counterevidence: Q3 2026 company operating income increased from $552m to $714m, but retail units grew faster, from 155,941 to 217,385. Operating income per retail unit fell from approximately $3,540 to $3,284. This is a companywide allocation, not site profitability, but it shows that more volume need not improve unit economics. ## 5. What would change our view?

- More production accompanied by aging ready inventory, markdowns, greater advertising or financing concessions would challenge profitable absorption.
- Higher labor hours without mix-adjusted completions, or persistently higher recon cost/rework, would challenge operational headroom.
- Turnover preventing certification progress would weaken the training-cohort upside.
- Openings, hiring or equipment delays would push output into later years.
- Improved per-car recon costs with healthy inventory turnover and stable contribution would strengthen the thesis.

## Pitch-ready version

“We underwrite further volume growth because a broader, faster-delivered selection can convert more demand, while Carvana still has identifiable ways to expand production within its existing property network. The strongest evidence is uneven historical labor recovery, line and shift ramps, additional ADESA integrations, and the potential maturation of recently hired workers—not empty parking lots. Our initial case gives no credit for future labor-productivity improvement. Profitable absorption and incremental cash returns remain conditions to test, not conclusions already established.”
