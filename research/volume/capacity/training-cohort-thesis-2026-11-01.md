# Hypothesis: hiring cohorts mature into higher-skilled production capacity

User-proposed volume thesis, November 1, 2026. Status: plausible mechanism; not a measured productivity uplift or demonstrated inflection.

## Mechanism

Hiring increases hours first. As retained employees progress through certification, they may perform more complex work, cover scarce skilled roles, require less supervision and make fewer errors. Consequently, effective output could grow faster than total hours without adding buildings. Training in bottleneck roles matters more than average tenure. Cross-training can improve attendance coverage and reduce queues elsewhere in the facility.

Citizens' June 2026 tour report describes approximately 1–3 years to move through Carvana's certification system, identifies labor/training as a recurring constraint, and discusses reminders for certification eligibility plus more accessible instructional material. This is not a claim that employees take 1–3 years to become productive, nor that everyone reaches a sudden productivity threshold. ## Correct the hiring timeline

Broad net workforce rebuilding is clearer in 2024–2025 than in 2023. Company year-end employment declined from 16,600 in 2022 to 13,700 in 2023, then increased to 17,400 in 2024 and 23,100 in 2025. These are companywide net counts, not gross recon hires or employee cohorts. ADESA acquisition also complicates earlier comparisons.

The 14-original-IRC panel independently shows annual average employees of 7,212 / 4,356 / 5,048 / 6,806 in 2022 / 2023 / 2024 / 2025. Individual hiring may have resumed during 2023 even while annual or year-end totals fell, but the broad 2023 rehiring assertion is not established.

Illustrative progression windows, not forecasts:
- 2023 hires: 2024–2026.
- 2024 hires: 2025–2027.
- 2025 hires: 2026–2028.

Thus the measured 2024–2025 expansion is consistent with a potential multi-year skill-development benefit during 2025–2028, including now. It does not establish a discrete 2026 inflection. Attrition, prior experience, job mix and certification participation determine how much of each hiring cohort remains and progresses.

## Why employment fell

Carvana shifted from rapid growth toward cost reduction as affordability worsened and demand weakened. Higher interest rates and elevated vehicle prices increased customer payments; financing-market changes also pressured loan-sale economics. Its Q3 2022 letter describes typical customer monthly payments up 22% versus 2021, and links loan-sale pricing to origination rates relative to benchmark rates. Cost reduction addressed a business built for more growth than materialized.

Approximately 2,500 reductions were announced in May 2022, primarily operational, and approximately 1,500 in November across corporate, technology and operations. These are not counts of skilled recon employees lost. Do not characterize this simply as a COVID shutdown layoff cycle or assume all laid-off workers were painters.

## Evidence that would support or undermine it

Support: certification completions and retained skilled-worker counts rise; comparable repair categories require fewer hours; output per hour improves at existing sites without extra equipment; first-pass quality improves; scarce-role vacancies or queues diminish.

Disconfirming/rival explanations: turnover offsets progression; output gains instead reflect newer/easier cars, additional hours or equipment; certification raises wages without changing bottleneck output; skilled staffing improves but another stage or demand limits production. Better average tenure alone is insufficient.

## Treatment in the volume framework

Keep the agreed constant-hours-per-vehicle case unchanged. This hypothesis is a separate potential productivity upside, currently unquantified. Do not add a training uplift on top of a ramp assumption already incorporating workforce maturation. Evidence must separate additional hours, cohort skill development, vehicle mix and new infrastructure.

Pitch wording: “Carvana's 2024–2025 workforce rebuilding may create a further productivity benefit as retained employees advance through its reported 1–3-year certification pathway. The timing is consistent with a 2025–2028 benefit, but its magnitude and effect on bottleneck output remain unmeasured.”
