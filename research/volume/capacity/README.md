**Research date: October 29, 2026. Financial endpoint: Q3 2026.** Free public evidence and existing project materials. [Sources and limitations](sources.md) · [Facility register](facility-register.md) · [Operational timeline](operating-timeline.md) · [Conditional scenarios](scenarios.md)

## Conclusion

Expanded October 30 source test: [Municipal staffing, Buffalo reporting, peer operations and training](source-test-2026-10-30.md). Adds an actual historical Trenton headcount and a missed employment commitment; preserves the distinction between staffing plans and measurable factory utilization.

October 30 follow-up: [Site ramps and operating bottlenecks](ramp-evidence-2026-10-30.md) adds a historical Carvana paint-line benchmark, technical process evidence and CarMax ramp comparisons. These support conditional stage-level calculations; site utilization remains unmeasured.

**The strongest explanation is expansion of executable reconditioning and fulfilment capacity within a large pre-existing property network—not a company that simply ran out of land or buildings.** ADESA added useful production locations, access to additional labor markets and local inventory pools. At the same time, Carvana had to staff facilities, develop managers and balance repair workloads. In 2026 it also deliberately selected newer vehicles that required less preparation. These mechanisms enabled more suitable inventory; their separate contributions to sales growth are not identifiable from public data.

The most revealing disclosure is the Q4 2024 call: the original IRC network already had potential capacity above **one million retail units annually**, before the ADESA integrations. The company sold **416,348** vehicles in 2024. This disproves the simple premise that an insufficient national building footprint necessarily prevented higher output. It does **not** prove the original network could promptly produce a million desirable vehicles at low cost, in the right places, with then-current staffing. [S1, S3](sources.md)

The bottleneck was most likely **how quickly Carvana could expand well-managed, cost-effective production and delivery**, with an explicit cost-control interruption after Q4 2025. Evidence is strongest for management/staffing, scheduling and the choice of repair workload. Evidence is weaker for parts shortages, acquisition scarcity, equipment shortage or delivery being the single binding stage. Management's assertion of ample profitable unmet demand is still unproven independently.

Physical runway appears substantial; a precise sales runway does not follow. The next phase mixes inexpensive activation of existing buildings with much larger construction projects. Growth can continue profitably if preparation and fulfilment scale without excessive concessions, repair costs or inventory aging. Q3 demonstrated growing aggregate profit, but **profit per retail unit declined**. Thus neither an immediate physical ceiling nor indefinite 40% profitable growth is supported.

## What changed physically—and what did not

| Route | Mechanism | Evidence for 2024–2026 | What remains to execute |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staff existing capacity | More productive people/shifts use existing lines | Explicit ongoing route; original network already substantial | Hiring, skill mix, supervision, maintenance, training and downstream transport |
| Integrate existing ADESA buildings | Install Carli workflows/equipment and adapt existing recon space | Six integrations at 2024 year-end, 16 at 2025 year-end, 21 at Q3 2026 | Commissioning, staffing, ramp and economic utilization |
| Build larger facilities on owned footprint | New/expanded structures and production lines | First full buildout began Q2 2026; three under construction Q3 for production in 2027 | Construction/permits, equipment, operational startup; not Q3 output |
| Greenfield development | Acquire/develop genuinely new sites | Original network included substantial earlier greenfields; Q1 2026 call says new greenfields not current priority | Site selection, land, approvals, utilities, construction and full workforce |

The acquisition closed **May 9, 2022 for approximately $2.2bn** and included 56 locations, approximately 6.5m square feet and over 4,000 acres. This was a wholesale business/property acquisition, not the cost of installing retail production. See [company acquisition disclosure](https://investors.carvana.com/news-releases/2022/05-10-2022-113013333). The later integrations activate part of that earlier investment; the original IRCs also represent an earlier capital cycle.

Management's August 2026 guidelines distinguish:

| Project | Incremental capex | Incremental annual capacity | Capex per unit of annual capacity, calculated |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Existing-site staffing | No incremental facility capex stated | Not quantified | Not calculable |
| Typical ADESA integration | $2–3m | ~13,000 | $154–231 |
| Full ADESA buildout | $30–35m | ~40,000 | $750–875 |

These are capacity-investment ratios, **not preparation costs per car**, and exclude acquisition cost, operating expense and working capital. At the guidelines, ten integrations imply ~130k incremental annual capacity and five imply ~65k, but neither is realized 2025/2026 output. Actual sites may differ, and openings during the year provide less than a full year's capacity. Three standard buildouts imply ~120k annual capacity and $90–105m total project spending; individual budgets and remaining spend are unknown. [S5](sources.md)

An independent check supports the larger-project distinction: Jacksonville regulatory reporting describes a new IRC building, retained wholesale structures and **$32m of site improvements** as of October 21. This resembles a full construction project economically, but is not confirmed as one of management's three buildouts. Its permit dates, differing plan/building areas and unverified production start are recorded separately. [S7](sources.md)

The named register contains **18 reconstructed original IRCs and 57 ADESA map entries**, with the candidate 21 integrations tagged. It is not a 75-factory operating census. The 57-entry map does not reconcile with the 56-property acquisition count; current status of the extra directory entry remains unresolved. Most per-site installed capacity, title/lease status and commissioning dates are not disclosed. These gaps are explicit in the [JSON register](facility-register.json), rather than filled using acreage or parking spaces.

No road-distance or population-coverage calculation was performed. Actual towns and addresses are available for future reproducible mapping, but circular catchments would imply a precision not supported by known operating starts and delivery routes. A site on the opposite side of a large metro may matter even if city-level coverage is unchanged.

There are still directory properties in large markets without confirmed retail integration in the Q3 roster—for example **Los Angeles/Mira Loma, San Diego and Washington/Dulles**—as well as smaller markets and overlapping locations such as San Antonio, Birmingham, Memphis and the Cincinnati–Dayton corridor. This shows heterogeneous optionality, not that every property is suitable or that larger metros necessarily come first. Jacksonville's long permitting sequence illustrates readiness constraints. Q2 management described balancing map gaps against where execution was best. Public evidence cannot rank the remaining properties' returns.

The Oklahoma City original IRC provides a useful concrete distinction: its 2022 opening report described over $40m invested, more than 150 people hired toward an eventual 350-plus, and storage for about 10,000 cars. A building can be completed before it is fully staffed, and parking capacity measures a stock rather than annual output. Most of those vehicles were expected to serve customers outside Oklahoma, illustrating why a facility's production need not map to sales in its immediate metro. [S7](sources.md)

ADESA sites also supported delivery before retail integration: May 2024 disclosure identifies last-mile operations at 30 locations and middle-mile at nine. Therefore non-integrated areas are not necessarily untreated logistics controls. Existing sites can serve more demand by deepening suitable inventory and increasing throughput; no new metro is required. **Geographic coverage, customer market share and production utilization are three different saturation questions.** [S3, S6](sources.md)

## What changed operationally

Effective output is set by the most restrictive stage: suitable acquisition → transport → inspection → repairs/paint → quality control → photography/release → delivery. A faster paint line does little if suitable acquisitions, skilled diagnostics or transport become the next bottleneck. Net first-pass yield also matters: inspection rejections and rework consume resources without creating sale-ready units.

The [quarterly timeline](operating-timeline.md) separates reported output from unknowns. Its main measurable anchors are **more than 40% production growth in 2025**, **36% companywide production growth in the written Q2 2026 chart**, and **more than 200,000 produced in Q3**, slightly above retail sales. The Q2 call uses inventory terminology for the regional chart; the written production label is retained with the conflict. There is no defensible numeric split between new facilities, same-site staffing, productivity and vehicle mix.

The Q4 2025 cost deterioration provides a useful operational test. Management specifically connected it to newer managers and smaller single-line sites. In early 2026 it prioritized cost recovery, revised staffing/paint-flow decisions and introduced more detailed management tools. By the April call, labor hours per vehicle had returned to the prior best Q2 2025 level, although absolute hours were not disclosed. New tools were not yet deployed at most sites: that limits attributing the whole recovery to software. [S3](sources.md)

The newer-car shift is substantive, not merely an accounting artifact. Q2 management explicitly linked newer, higher-priced acquisitions to less reconditioning and faster inventory expansion. Prime-focused financing improvements simultaneously shifted demand; higher-income sales increased more than 60%, while management said the mix crowded out some cheaper cars. Q3 commentary still described later-model, higher-price and prime emphasis. The intended broadening was therefore **not demonstrated as complete**. No age/mileage distribution or within-category repair-hours series was found. [S3, S4](sources.md)

Returning to older cars can broaden budget coverage, but increase repair hours, parts variability, queue time and rework; lower acquisition cost can be offset by longer holding periods. If repair labor is binding, unchanged productive hours with **10% more hours per car means 9.1% fewer completed cars**; 20% more means 16.7% fewer. Those are hypothetical sensitivities, not measured mix effects. Cheaper cars could sell faster or slower depending on price, credit and selection. Their lower price alone does not determine working capital or margin.

Retail revenue per unit cannot identify this mix cleanly. The transition from net marketplace revenue to gross revenue for some partner vehicles, plus fees and other mix, affects the denominator. Use management's explicit vehicle-mix statements as directional evidence, not a fabricated average vehicle-age series.

The evidence supports an **operational supply limitation relative to management's desired growth pace**. It does not establish universal excess demand at unchanged prices, nor isolate a causal ADESA percentage. Regional production-sales correlations cannot resolve reverse causation, allocation to stronger markets or national inventory spillovers; no elasticity is used here.

## Is added output generating acceptable returns?

Companywide outcomes are consistent with profitable expansion but weaker unit economics:

| Q3 measure | 2025 | 2026 | Interpretation |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Retail units | 155,941 | 217,385 | +39.4% |
| GAAP retail GPU | $3,456 | $3,556 | +$100; not mix-adjusted recon efficiency |
| Wholesale GPU, per retail unit | $866 | $708 | Denominator/allocation includes separate wholesale business |
| Other GPU | $3,040 | $2,733 | Financing/product economics matter for total contribution |
| Total GPU | $7,362 | $6,997 | −$365 |
| GAAP operating income | $552m | $714m | +29.3%, slower than units |
| Operating income / retail unit, calculated | $3,540 | $3,284 | −$255; company allocation, not marginal site return |
| Advertising / retail unit, calculated | $654 | $699 | Higher spending to support offering/awareness; not a traffic analysis |

Nine-month 2026 operating cash flow was **$900m** versus $606m; after property/equipment purchases, **$734m** versus $510m. This simple cash measure excludes acquisitions and other investing/financing flows and is not attributable to new IRCs. Inventory consumed $1.025bn versus $676m. Higher absolute profit/cash cannot prove attractive incremental capacity returns. [Q3 financial statements, S2](sources.md)

Capex categories must remain separate: $2.2bn acquisition; $2–3m integrations; $30–35m buildouts; maintenance/technology/other facilities; hiring/training expensed in operations; and inventory/receivables funding. Company property/equipment purchases rose from **$91m in 2024 to $147m in 2025**, then **$166m in nine months 2026 versus $96m**. No public reconciliation allocates these totals cleanly to IRC integration, larger builds, maintenance, logistics and technology. Do not label the entire amount ADESA investment.

Working capital may be much larger than low-cost integrations suggest. At an **assumed** $22,000 carried vehicle cost and 45 inventory days, every additional **100,000 annual sales requires about $271m of vehicle stock**; at 60 days, ~$362m. At one million annual sales, 15 extra holding days tie up ~$904m. These are sensitivities, not company inventory turns or borrowing forecasts. Payables, financing, mix and the distinction between ready stock and work-in-process change the actual cash requirement.

An incremental vehicle must cover acquisition/preparation, transport, variable service and marketing costs, financing concessions and expected returns/markdowns. Then incremental fixed costs, depreciation, capital and taxes must earn a return. Illustratively, 100k additional sales at $1k/$2k/$3k contribution before incremental fixed costs generate $100m/$200m/$300m. But $500 less contribution across one million sales costs $500m—requiring 250k extra sales at $2k contribution just to offset it. None of these margins is an estimated Carvana site margin.

## Conditional runway, not capacity-as-sales forecasts

The reproducible scenarios normalize starting annual output to **800k**, deliberately below the disclosed Q3 annualized threshold; the exact starting output is unknown. This is not a forecast baseline to import unadjusted into an earnings model. Existing-site gains, future-project output, repair mix and sales absorption are separate parameters. All output paths below remain below 1.5m; this is a plausibility boundary, not a verified installed-capacity utilization ceiling.

| Case | 2027 / 2028 / 2029 potential output | Required conditions | Gross project capital and economic implication | Invalidation signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Existing footprint | 920k / 1,030k / 1,133k | Existing-site output +15%, +12%, +10%; stable repair intensity; full sales absorption | No new construction assumed; hiring/logistics and ~$904m incremental steady-state vehicle stock by 2029 still required | Comparable-mix productivity stagnates, staffing fails or ready stock ages |
| Expansion supported | 979k / 1,199k / 1,322k | Same existing gains; three 40k buildouts plus six hypothetical 13k integrations; 30% first-year, 85%, 95% realization | $102–123m gross project budgets, not remaining spend; ~$1.42bn incremental steady-state vehicle stock by 2029 | Commissioning slips, ramp cost persists or sales require margin erosion |
| Operational downside | 797k / 885k / 950k | Productive resources +8%, +5%, +5%; repair hours/car +10%; slower three-buildout ramp; no extra integrations | $90–105m buildout budgets with worse utilization; higher unit costs could outweigh fixed-cost leverage | Strong mix-adjusted HPU gains and timely staffing would improve this case |
| Demand/economic downside | Same potential supply as expansion case; absorbable sales 930k / 1,079k / 1,123k | Demand at acceptable economics absorbs 95%, 90%, 85% of potential supply | Curtail output or take concessions; failed 2027 curtailment leaves ~49k vehicles, ~$1.08bn at assumed cost | Sell-through and contribution remain healthy without further concessions |

The six 2027 integrations are **assumed**, not the company's 2026 guidance rolled forward. No new 2028–29 cohort is assumed. Full-buildout and integration additions are separate from staffing/productivity on the starting footprint. Full project budgets may include money already spent in 2026. The hypothetical stock requirements are based on 45 days and are not added again to unsold-stock stress. See [full assumptions and annual results](scenarios.md).

These scenarios show why meaningful further growth is physically conceivable, but why buildings alone provide little confidence in next year's growth rate. Quantitative profitability and ROIC by facility cannot be estimated defensibly without contribution, ramp expense and allocation data.

## Monitoring and what can enter the model

1. **Quarterly completed output with consistent definition**, plus new versus existing sites. This is the direct supply driver; retail sales are not a substitute.
2. **HPU and preparation cost within age/mileage/repair categories**, alongside first-pass quality and queues. Needed to separate execution from easier vehicles.
3. **Active staffed lines, shifts and manager training**, not just property/site counts. Watch whether mature sites continue expanding.
4. **2027 commissioning dates and project spend** for the three larger builds; reconcile capacity already embedded in 1.5m before adding it again.
5. **Immediately ready owned inventory, aging and markdowns**, with marketplace/returns bridge. Production ahead of sales is constructive only if stock remains desirable and turns well.
6. **Contribution after financing concessions, advertising and delivery**, not just retail GPU or aggregate EBITDA. Watch restoration of broader mix at stable comparable margins.
7. **Inventory cash use and funding alongside capex**, distinguishing price/mix from units and operating versus financial sources of cash.

**Model-ready:** disclosed integration counts, reported company financials, project cost/capacity guidelines as conditional inputs, and explicitly hypothetical scenarios. **Not model-ready:** an ADESA causal sales uplift, a numeric same-site/new-site production split, individual facility utilization, mix-adjusted productivity, local saturation curves or a measured site ROIC. The data gap is now specific; further weak proxies would not resolve it.
