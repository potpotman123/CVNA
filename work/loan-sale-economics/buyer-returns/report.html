Carvana: can the loan buyer earn an adequate return?

Evidence and sensitivity analysis • 30 October 2026 • CRVNA 2026-P2

Verdict: a meaningful securitization premium is economically plausible. We have not validated Carvana’s companywide gain ratio or established subsidized buying. New public pricing evidence reduces the unidentified portion of one transaction. A monthly cash-flow model shows that the residual buyer can earn a double-digit return under some assumptions, but that return is sensitive to losses, prepayments and the gap between borrower rates and funding coupons.

This is an economic sensitivity model, not a reconstruction of the private buyer’s underwriting case or a valuation opinion. The former CarMax 2.79% companywide benchmark remains withdrawn.

1. What changed in the evidence

The filed pricing communication marks the transaction “PRICED” and states a $49.272 million value for the entire R class, $46.8084 million offered, and an 18.5% modeled yield using the private certificate memorandum’s Case 2. It also prices N near par. This is stronger evidence than an undisclosed residual assumption, but remains a pricing communication rather than a complete closing settlement. The private Case 2 assumptions and XS sale terms were not recovered. Filed pricing sheet.

A buyer independently reports a P2 residual position: Eagle Point Institutional Income Fund lists $1.431844 million cost, $1.424959 million fair value and a 13.10% estimated effective yield at June 30. That yield is not the pricing sheet’s 18.5% scenario and neither is a realized return. Different modeling, timing or amortized-cost conventions could explain the difference; the disclosures do not resolve it. It must not be silently treated as one agreed hurdle rate. Investor schedule and footnote 6.

2. Transaction structure and consideration

Carvana LLC / Carvana FAC originate loans → depositor → issuing and grantor trusts → borrower collections allocated among servicing/XS, rated A–D notes, N and residual certificates. Bridgecrest collects payments; ordinary credit losses fall on transaction cash flows and ultimately investors according to priority. Representation breaches and servicing obligations are separate from ordinary default risk. Carvana retains risk interests; public underwriters are not thereby identified as private whole-loan buyers. 3. Cash-flow model and assumptions

We use the final prospectus’s 67 illustrative subpools, retaining their balance, rate and term distributions. Their balances reconcile exactly to $1,100,395,400.20. This avoids the unresolved cutoff reconciliation in the earlier loan-level snapshot. As a definition cross-check, all 38,690 initial loan records have an original-amount-weighted contractual rate of 10.539%, close to the disclosed 10.54% APR. It does not establish identity of rate and APR on every loan.

Each month records scheduled principal, interest, defaults, voluntary prepayments, lagged net recoveries, fees and remaining balances. The unlevered valuation discounts those net asset cash flows without subtracting financing costs. The separate waterfall pays the 1% servicing strip, expenses, A–D interest and interleaved parity cures, reserve top-ups and the 0.9% overcollateralization target, then N interest and 65% of remaining cash toward N principal; the residual receives the remainder. XS is separated from the 0.45% base fee. Reserves are funded once and their releases are allocated to the correct owner.

ABS speed here includes defaults as part of unscheduled runoff. The model allocates a shaped default hazard within total attrition, then attributes the remaining attrition to voluntary repayments; it does not apply historical all-cause runoff and then add defaults again. Net losses are calibrated as gross defaults less delayed recoveries, not deducted a second time from cash flow.

Limitations: monthly regular periods replace actual settlement and first-payment stubs; fees are simplified; no optional 2% cleanup call, legal acceleration or investor fund leverage is modeled. No tested scenario has note-interest shortfalls, but this is not a rating stress engine. Exact timing and private Case 2 inputs can materially affect R valuation. No attempt was made to fit assumptions to force agreement with the observed R price.

At a hypothetical price of 107.40, the central unlevered buyer return is 5.36%. At 105 it is 6.52%. A 7.4% companywide accounting gain is not evidence that this pool sold at 107.40. This exercise merely shows why the required return matters as much as borrower APR.

Securitization can yield a different aggregate value because most funding is sold as protected senior securities, while a much smaller residual earns a high required return. The R investor does not pay for the entire loan pool. Do not demand an 18.5% return on every dollar of whole-loan principal or subtract the note coupons again from an unlevered valuation.

5. Performance checks and disconfirming evidence

The September collection report shows a $956.172 million ending pool, 86.89% of cutoff principal, 0.06% cumulative net losses and 0.18% greater-than-60-day delinquency. It paid about $1.225 million to R and $0.451 million to XS. These are observed reported distributions, not independently audited borrower receipts or annualized returns. The pool is too young to validate lifetime losses. September servicer report, distributed October 13.

Actual runoff is faster than the model’s five-full-month central balance of 88.78%. Closing stubs and collection timing prevent a precise one-number comparison, but this is a reason to retain the faster-runoff downside, not discard it.

Historical final-prospectus Annex I data provide a broader check: 2021-P1 was at 1.53% cumulative net loss after 60 periods; 2022-P3 at 3.46% after 43; 2023-P3 at 3.72% after 32; and 2024-P1 at 1.55% after 25. These are different vintages with different selection and remaining lives. They support a wide loss range, not a claim that 3% is safely conservative. Secondary reproductions of rating opinions cited roughly 2.75–3.0% expectations; the original rating body was inaccessible, so those figures were not treated as independent primary validation.

Checks separate arithmetic from validation: exact cohort-principal reconciliation; an analytical zero-loss/zero-fee loan discounts to par at its contractual yield; monthly principal and waterfall cash conservation; sensible scenario direction; and independent reconstruction of the actual September $2.276 million N principal and $1.225 million residual distributions to cents. The single-average-loan alternative changes the modeled whole-loan price at a 7% hurdle by only about $0.06 per $100 in this scenario. None of these checks validates the assumed lifetime losses.

6. Servicing and companywide implications

Raising the base servicing fee from 0.45% to 0.51% reduces central whole-loan value by about $0.12 per $100 at a 7% required return. Raising it to 1% reduces value by about $1.13. Within this trust’s fixed 1% strip, the same increase takes value from XS, leaving R unchanged in the modeled normal waterfall. It would be incorrect to count the fee saving both as higher R value and as XS proceeds. The earlier P3 0.39%/0.45% successor comparison is context, not the fee contract for this P2 deal or for Ally.

The nine-month accounting bridge remains $14.722bn cash plus $266m new retained value minus approximately $13.904bn implied net basis = $1.084bn gain. This is an accounting consistency check, not an independent fair-price test. Q3’s blended gain ratio is approximately 7.40%, broadly flat against Q2, and cannot be assigned to P2. Q3 2026 filing.

Every one-point change in sale consideration on Q3’s $5.15bn principal is approximately $51.5m of gain sensitivity, all else equal; on nine-month principal it is $139m. For the securitization channel alone, one point on $3.7bn is $37m. Do not apply this P2 loss or rate shock mechanically to all channels or describe those amounts as forecast losses. Volume, underwriting, capitalized costs, financing penetration and vehicle contribution can offset or amplify the effect.

Base-case eligible: observed sale volumes, reported gains, identified contractual terms and filed pricing references. Sensitivity only: whole-loan prices, residual returns, servicing-value effects and profit exposures. Context only: historical vintage outcomes and investor effective-yield estimates. Unresolved: a normalized companywide fair gain ratio, an Ally/private-pool return estimate, or a quantified subsidy adjustment.

The smallest useful next evidence is the P2 certificate Case 2 cash-flow assumptions plus the XS ownership/settlement and the transaction carrying-basis bridge. For the private channels, one dated settlement and the matched purchased cohort are more valuable than additional public APR averages. This pass stops at that boundary: no paid collection, gated-material bypass or invented private pricing.

Investment conclusion: the evidence is stronger for “Carvana can earn genuine origination/securitization value, but the amount is cyclical and assumption-sensitive” than for either “all elevated gains are suspicious” or “the entire reported gain ratio is validated and sustainable.”
