PART A — PHYSICAL CAPACITY, OPERATIONAL CAPACITY AND PROFITABLE VOLUME

1. HYPOTHESES TO TEST

Bullish mechanism: substantial installed infrastructure was underused after the contraction; staffing, shifts, management capability and processes subsequently improved, increasing desirable sale-ready inventory and profitable sales without proportional construction spending.

Alternatives and qualifications:
- Growth required substantial new equipment or buildings rather than simply reactivating existing capacity.
- Newer, easier-to-recondition vehicles increased measured throughput without comparable within-category productivity gains.
- Demand, financing concessions, sourcing changes or price reductions drove sales, with production following demand.
- Historical staffing was inefficient; restoring old headcount would not produce attractive incremental returns.
- Available physical space exists in the wrong markets or lacks the skilled labor and logistics required to use it.
- Network complexity, training and quality rework constrain ramp speed even where land and buildings are abundant.
- Much of the old slack has already been consumed by late 2026.
